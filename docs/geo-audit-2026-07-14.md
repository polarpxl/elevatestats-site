# GEO Audit: elevatestats.app

**Date:** July 14, 2026
**Scope:** Generative Engine Optimization (how AI systems like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Copilot discover, parse, extract, and cite this site). This is an audit only. Nothing has been changed.

**How this was verified:** every page component and content file in the repo was read, the live site was fetched with GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot user agents, the live robots.txt and sitemap.xml were pulled, every internal link on the main pages was tested, and the recommendations below were checked against the Next.js 16.2.4 documentation bundled in this repo plus current Next.js docs via context7 (the current guidance is still: inline `<script type="application/ld+json">` blocks in page components, and the Metadata export with `alternates.canonical`; there is no newer structured data API to adopt).

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **The site is in good shape for AI crawlers.** Nothing blocks GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot (verified live, all return 200). The sitemap is complete with honest dates, every page has exactly one H1 and a canonical tag, content is server rendered, support articles are genuinely excellent, and JSON-LD already exists on the three highest value pages. There are no broken internal links. This audit found real gaps but no disasters.
- **Thing that matters most #1: the site never plainly says what the product is.** There is no visible sentence like "Elevate Stats is a hockey stat tracking app for amateur coaches" anywhere. Headings are slogans ("The rink is waiting"). AI systems cite plain definitional sentences; the closest one lives hidden inside a JSON-LD block.
- **Thing that matters most #2: the product has two names.** Marketing pages and page titles say "Elevate Hockey Stats" while legal pages, emails, and half the structured data say "Elevate Stats". The two JSON-LD blocks on the homepage even disagree with each other. Name inconsistency is the single biggest cause of AI systems getting an entity wrong or splitting it into two.
- **Thing that matters most #3: key facts are invisible until a user clicks.** On /pricing, the yearly price ($109 CAD) and all nine FAQ answers are not in the page HTML on load; they only appear after tapping a toggle or an accordion. The FAQ is rescued by its JSON-LD copy, the yearly price is not rescued on that page at all.
- **The biggest long term lever is missing content, not broken code.** The site cannot answer the questions coaches actually type into AI assistants ("best hockey stat tracking app for minor hockey", "free hockey stats app", "Elevate Stats vs paper game sheets"). The FAQ is billing only. There are no comparison, use case, or answer pages.

**Checked and fine (no action needed):**

- **AI crawler access:** robots.txt allows all user agents (which covers GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-User, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Google-Extended, Bingbot, CCBot, Applebot-Extended, and Meta-ExternalAgent by the wildcard). Live fetches with GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot user agents all returned 200. There is no middleware, no vercel.json, and no firewall rule in the repo that could block bots. One caveat: Vercel dashboard level firewall or bot protection settings cannot be seen from the repo, so confirm in the Vercel dashboard that Attack Mode and bot filtering are off or exempt AI crawlers.
- **Sitemap:** present, complete for all indexable pages, referenced from robots.txt, with honest per article lastmod dates (a deliberate fixed date scheme, which is better than fake build time freshness).
- **/parents excluded from the sitemap and marked noindex:** intentional (it is a paid traffic landing page) and correctly implemented.
- **Rendering:** everything except the two /pricing issues above is in the server rendered HTML, including all testimonials, feature lists, the signup form, and full article bodies. No content is trapped in images; icons are aria hidden, decorative images have empty alt text, and screenshots have descriptive alt text.
- **Pricing consistency:** $14 CAD/month and $109 CAD/year are correct in 100 percent of live occurrences, and the derived numbers ($59 savings, $9.08/month equivalent) are mathematically right everywhere they appear. The free tier is always "Free", the trial is always "3 games" (never day based), and no stale price exists anywhere in shipped code. This is genuinely rare and worth protecting.
- **Company relationship in prose:** "Elevate Stats is operated by Elevate Sports Labs Inc." is stated plainly in the privacy policy, terms, email footer, and About page. It just is not in the structured data yet (finding #3).
- **Support articles:** the strongest GEO asset on the site. Server rendered MDX, clean H1 then H2 outline with anchor links, visible "Updated {date}" plus read time, per article titles, descriptions, and canonicals via generateMetadata, and TechArticle plus BreadcrumbList JSON-LD with dateModified.
- **Technical hygiene:** canonical tags on every page, titles and descriptions on every page, Open Graph image with alt text, www and http both redirect cleanly (single hop 308) to https://elevatestats.app, and a crawl of every internal link on the main pages found zero broken links and no redirect chains. Nothing about Core Web Vitals looked broken enough to matter here.

## 3. Prioritized fix list

### Quick wins (under an hour each)

1. **Add a definitional sentence to the homepage** (finding 1). One copy change to the hero subhead, for example: "Elevate Stats is a hockey stat tracking app that lets amateur coaches capture, share, and learn from pro quality game stats, right from the bench." This also starts resolving the naming split in the most visible spot.
2. **Fix the homepage JSON-LD entities** (findings 3 and 9). Model Elevate Sports Labs Inc. as the Organization, name the product once, link them with `publisher`, and replace `billingDuration` with a valid `priceSpecification`. Ready to paste draft in section 4.
3. **Fix "Pro" to "PRO" in the terms** (finding 8). Three lines in `terms.mdx`.
4. **Add "CAD" to the pricing meta description** (finding 12). One line.
5. **Add CSV export to the PRO list in free-vs-pro-explained** (finding 13). One line.
6. **Add llms.txt** (finding 7). A single static text file in `public/`. No new dependency, no new pattern. Draft in section 4.
7. **Escape `<` in the three JSON-LD stringify calls** (finding 17). Mechanical.

### Worth doing

8. **Decide the canonical product name and reconcile** (finding 2). This is a business decision first: is the product "Elevate Stats" or "Elevate Hockey Stats"? Recommendation: make "Elevate Stats" the entity name everywhere (it matches the domain, the legal docs, and the emails) and keep "hockey stats" in titles as description rather than name, for example "Elevate Stats: pro level hockey stats for amateur coaches". Then sweep titles, nav, footer, and schema to match.
9. **Render FAQ answers in the initial HTML** (finding 4). Change the accordion so the answer markup is always in the DOM and only visually hidden (a `<details>`/`<summary>` element is the simplest honest fix). Keep the FAQPage JSON-LD.
10. **Get the yearly price into the /pricing initial HTML** (finding 5). Options: render both periods server side and toggle visibility with CSS, or add a static line under the PRO card ("$14 CAD/month, or $109 CAD/year (save $59)"). Either way the number must exist before any click.
11. **Fix the heading outline** (finding 10): wrap the plan grid in an H2 on /pricing, promote compact feature names on /features to H3, give the founder block on /about a real heading.
12. **Expand the FAQ toward discovery language** (finding 6, first step). Add the drafted questions in section 4 to `faqs.ts`, and consider surfacing the FAQ (or a subset) on the homepage, not just /pricing. FAQPage schema already generates from this file automatically.
13. **Add Open Graph and Twitter metadata to /privacy and /terms** (finding 15).
14. **Rewrite the most load bearing slogan headings as claims** (finding 11). Feature category headings and how-it-works are the priority; brand moments like "The rink is waiting" can stay.

### Nice to have

15. **Comparison and use case pages** (finding 6, the full version). One page per real question: "Elevate Stats vs paper game sheets", "Elevate Stats vs spreadsheet stat tracking", "How to track shots on goal for a minor hockey team", "Hockey stat tracking for parents". This is the largest lever on the list but it is content writing, not engineering.
16. **External corroboration** (finding 14): fill in the social profile URLs when accounts are live, get listed in app and product directories, and pursue reviews or press that link back. Then add those URLs as `sameAs` in the Organization schema.
17. **WebSite JSON-LD** in the layout. Note: a SearchAction should not be added because the site has no search feature; claiming one in schema would be false.
18. **llms-full.txt**: a concatenated plain text version of the key pages and support articles. Worth doing only after the content itself is settled; it could be generated at build time from the same MDX sources (this would be a small build script, flagged here as a new, minor pattern for approval).
19. **Tidy the small stuff**: findings 16, 18, 19.

**Dependency and pattern flags (per your rules, nothing added without approval):**

- No fix above requires a new npm dependency. The optional `schema-dts` package would add TypeScript checking for JSON-LD blocks; nice, not needed, and it is a dev only dependency. Flagged for your call.
- llms.txt is a new static file, not a new pattern. llms-full.txt generation (item 18) would introduce a small build script, which is a new pattern; approve before implementing.
- Everything else uses APIs already in use in this repo (Metadata export, inline JSON-LD scripts), confirmed current for Next.js 16.2.4 against the bundled docs and context7.

### 4d. Proposed new FAQ questions

These use the language coaches and parents actually type into AI assistants and search boxes. Suggested answers are drafted for review; adjust facts before shipping anything. Adding them to `src/lib/faqs.ts` automatically includes them in the existing FAQPage schema on /pricing.

1. **What is Elevate Stats?**
   Elevate Stats is a hockey stat tracking app for amateur coaches. You track shots, goals, and game events from the bench on your phone or tablet, and get shot maps, player and goalie stats, and AI coaching insights after every game.

2. **Is there a free hockey stat tracking app? Is Elevate Stats free?**
   Yes. The Free tier includes unlimited team level game tracking forever, plus 3 full PRO games to try everything. No credit card is required to start.

3. **How do I track shots on goal for a minor hockey team?**
   Open Elevate Stats during the game and tap the rink where the shot happened. Each tap records the shot location, and PRO attributes it to a player. After the game you get a shot map and shooting stats automatically.

4. **Does Elevate Stats work for minor hockey, beer league, and youth teams?**
   Yes. It is built specifically for amateur hockey: minor and youth teams, junior, and adult recreational leagues. It is designed to be usable by one person on a cold bench with gloves on.

5. **Does it work at rinks with no cell signal?**
   Yes. Tracking works fully offline and syncs automatically when your connection returns.

6. **Do I need to install an app from the App Store?**
   No. Elevate Stats is a web app that runs in your browser at app.elevatestats.app, and you can add it to your home screen so it behaves like a native app.

7. **What does Elevate Stats cost?**
   The Free tier is $0 forever. PRO is $14 CAD per month or $109 CAD per year (which saves $59 a year). Clubs, associations, and leagues can contact us for volume pricing.

8. **Can parents see their player's stats?**
   Yes. Coaches can share stats, and parents can also track games themselves from the stands to follow their player's progress across a season.

9. **How is Elevate Stats different from a paper game sheet or a spreadsheet?**
   A paper sheet captures totals; Elevate Stats captures where and when every event happened, then turns that into shot maps, trends, and AI insights with no manual data entry after the game.

Question 9 is also the seed for the comparison pages in recommendation 15: each comparison question that gets asked a lot deserves its own page, because AI assistants cite dedicated pages over FAQ fragments.
